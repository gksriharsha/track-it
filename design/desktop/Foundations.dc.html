Desktop shell — what changed and why

Four fixes, one system

Nothing here is a new aesthetic — it is the existing tokens (warm neutrals, one green accent, the terracotta reserved for a limit actually exceeded, Newsreader for numerals) applied with one more degree of hierarchy: tone for structure, not just for state.

--chrome is the only new token: a shade past --sunken, used only behind the sidebar and nowhere in the content pane. It says "structure" the way a mail app's source list differs from its reading pane — tone doing the work that a colour change would have overdone. The sidebar's selected nav row also stops inverting to solid ink-on-cream and instead uses --accent-wash + a 3px accent rule, the same restraint the app already applies to "over the limit": a tint, not a block of colour, carries the meaning.

Both put a bare ‹ in the leading corner, at the same size, in the same weight. The corner is the one position this app otherwise reserves for "go back" — so the day-stepper reads as a return action it is not.

The stepper moves out of the corner into a bounded, bordered control — its shape now says "adjust a value," not "return." A single reusable circled-chevron becomes the app's one Back affordance everywhere, replacing the two treatments currently in use — a text link on Supplements, and the same quiet full-word button repeated on both Vessels and Custom foods — worth carrying into those three screens next.

Space — the arithmetic

A 940px content column centred in a 1440px window leaves roughly 250px bare on each side — a third of the window doing nothing. The sidebar (256px) and a two-column top row put almost all of that width to work: the day's headline card and "Worth a look" sit side by side instead of one stacked above a mostly-empty full-width card, and "Add food" gets a fixed, always-visible spot instead of sharing a row with four unrelated destinations.
